# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Recruiters and hiring engineers looking for data science, machine learning or security interns and junior hires. They often skim on a phone, in a few seconds, between other candidates. Some are non-engineers screening for fit; some are engineers checking whether the work is real.

## Product Purpose

The personal portfolio of Tobias Anuf, a final-year Information Engineering student in Asunción, Paraguay. It exists to get him interviews for remote or hybrid roles. Success means a visitor emails him or opens his resume.

## Positioning

Focus, in his own order: data science, machine learning and cybersecurity (which he is learning). Computer vision stays as proof and as the last keyword, not the headline.

His work is shipped and measured rather than coursework: a self-driving Raspberry Pi car that runs on real camera input, and an internship model that recovered masked importer IDs in national customs data, validated against Central Bank statistics. He combines hardware (Raspberry Pi, Jetson Nano, Wio Terminal), computer vision and data science, with international experience from a year and a half at NTUST in Taipei.

## Operating Context

- A single static page on GitHub Pages: https://tobiascca.github.io/personal-portfolio/
- Resumes are built from LaTeX sources in `resume/` (EN and ES) and published as `resume_TobiasAnuf_EN.pdf` and `resume_TobiasAnuf_ES.pdf` at the repo root.
- Visitors arrive from the resume, LinkedIn and GitHub links.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- One scrolling page, no subpages.
- Dark background.
- The lane-detection demo video stays in the first screen and keeps playing on loop.
- EN and ES resume PDFs stay prominent.
- Open to remote or hybrid roles.
- Undecided: expected graduation date (the user will supply it); the Chess project visual (the user will supply a clip or screenshot).

## Brand Commitments

- Name: Tobias Anuf.
- Voice: human and plain, never performative; the user rejected a polished sign-off ("Write to me") and a pitchy hero. Plain enough for a non-engineer recruiter. Technical detail belongs further down, near its evidence, not in the hero. The user rejected an overly specific hero description.
- Must not feel gimmicky, corporate, too technical, or hard to read.

## Product Principles

1. Show the work moving before describing it.
2. Every claim has a number or a link behind it.
3. A recruiter should know who he is, what he does and how to reach him within one screen.
4. Readability on a phone outranks effect.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast. Respect reduced motion except for the hero demo video, which the user wants always playing (a visible pause control keeps it accessible).
